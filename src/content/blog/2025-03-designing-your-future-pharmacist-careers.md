In the fast-evolving world of healthcare, pharmacists face numerous challenges — from adapting to digital transformations to navigating regulatory changes. Bill Burnett and Dave Evans' book, *Designing Your Life*, offers a refreshing perspective on how pharmacists can apply design thinking to both their personal and professional journeys. By integrating these principles, pharmacists can build fulfilling careers while maintaining work-life balance and professional satisfaction.

## 1. Reframing pharmacy careers as a design challenge

Pharmacists often feel constrained by traditional career paths, whether in retail, hospital, or industry settings. Burnett and Evans encourage individuals to treat their careers as design challenges rather than linear paths. By embracing this mindset, pharmacists can explore alternative roles such as digital health, consultancy, or entrepreneurship.

## 2. Prototyping career options

A core principle of *Designing Your Life* is prototyping — testing career choices before making long-term commitments. Pharmacists can apply this by:

- Shadowing professionals in non-traditional roles like pharmaceutical logistics or regulatory affairs.
- Taking short-term courses in emerging fields such as telepharmacy or artificial intelligence in healthcare.
- Volunteering in public health initiatives to explore new interests.

## 3. The power of "wayfinding"

Wayfinding is about making career decisions without a rigid long-term plan but rather through continuous small steps. Pharmacists can use this approach by:

- Attending industry conferences to identify future trends.
- Engaging in networking and mentorship opportunities.
- Continuously upskilling through certifications and workshops.

## 4. Work-life balance through energy mapping

Pharmacists often face burnout due to long hours and demanding environments. Burnett and Evans suggest using "energy mapping" to identify tasks that energise or drain an individual. Pharmacists can apply this by:

- Delegating administrative tasks where possible.
- Seeking roles that align with their interests, such as patient counselling for those who enjoy direct interaction.
- Incorporating mindfulness and wellness practices into daily routines.

## 5. Failing forward: embracing setbacks

Failure is often seen negatively, but *Designing Your Life* teaches that setbacks are opportunities for growth. Pharmacists who have faced challenges — whether in business ventures, failed job applications, or workplace issues — can use these experiences to refine their career paths.

## 6. Building a supportive community

The authors emphasise the importance of "co-designing" life with others. Pharmacists can benefit from:

- Joining professional associations and networking groups.
- Seeking mentorship from experienced colleagues.
- Collaborating with interdisciplinary teams to enhance patient care.
