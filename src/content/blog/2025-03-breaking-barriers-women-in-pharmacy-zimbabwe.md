International Women's Day is a time to celebrate the achievements of women across the globe and to acknowledge their contributions in various fields. In Zimbabwe, female pharmacists play an indispensable role in shaping the healthcare landscape, ensuring access to quality medicines, advocating for public health, and driving innovation in pharmaceutical practice.

Today, we recognise their resilience, expertise, and dedication to advancing the pharmacy profession despite numerous challenges.

Historically, pharmacy was a male-dominated profession, but over the years, women have broken barriers and assumed leadership roles in academia, industry, regulatory affairs, and community practice. Female pharmacists have not only excelled in their professional duties but have also contributed significantly to policy development, research, and mentorship, paving the way for future generations of women in pharmacy.

Despite the challenges of gender bias and limited leadership opportunities, many female pharmacists in Zimbabwe have risen to top positions in government, regulatory bodies, and pharmaceutical organisations. Their leadership in institutions such as the Medicines Control Authority of Zimbabwe (MCAZ), the Pharmacists Council of Zimbabwe (PCZ), and the Pharmaceutical Society of Zimbabwe (PSZ) has influenced policies that strengthen the pharmacy profession.

## Champions of public health and patient care

Women in pharmacy have been instrumental in spearheading initiatives that enhance patient care, medication safety, and accessibility to essential medicines. From running community pharmacies to working in hospital settings, female pharmacists ensure that patients receive appropriate pharmaceutical care. Their dedication is evident in rural and urban areas alike, where they educate communities on medication use, disease prevention, and health promotion.

Moreover, Zimbabwean women in pharmacy have embraced digitalisation and technological advancements to streamline supply chains, enhance pharmaceutical logistics, and improve patient outcomes. They have also played a pivotal role in research and development, contributing to innovations in pharmaceutical compounding and drug manufacturing.

## Overcoming challenges

Female pharmacists face several challenges, including workplace discrimination, limited access to funding for entrepreneurial ventures, and the struggle to balance professional and personal responsibilities. Despite these obstacles, they continue to excel, advocating for better working conditions, mentorship programmes, and policies that support gender equity in the pharmaceutical sector.

## A call for continued support and recognition

As we celebrate International Women's Day, it is essential to recognise and support the contributions of female pharmacists in Zimbabwe. The government, private sector, and professional associations should continue to promote gender equality, offer leadership training, and provide opportunities for women to thrive in pharmacy.

## Inspiring the next generation

The impact of female pharmacists extends beyond their professional duties. Through mentorship programmes, career guidance, and capacity-building initiatives, they inspire young women to pursue pharmacy as a career. Their involvement in training programmes, such as the Zimbabwe Pharmaceutical Students Association (ZPSA) and early career pharmacist training, ensures that the next generation of pharmacists is well-equipped to meet the demands of the profession.

> "To all the incredible women in pharmacy, we honour your dedication, passion, and commitment to improving healthcare in Zimbabwe. Your contributions are invaluable, and your impact will continue to shape the future of the pharmaceutical profession for years to come. Happy International Women's Day!"
