The COVID-19 pandemic highlighted the critical role of pharmacists in public health, demonstrating their ability to enhance healthcare delivery beyond traditional dispensing roles. In Zimbabwe, where public health challenges such as infectious diseases, non-communicable diseases (NCDs), and limited healthcare accessibility persist, pharmacists should be granted expanded roles in public health initiatives. This article explores why pharmacists need to be more involved in public health and outlines key areas where their expertise can significantly improve health outcomes.

## The role of pharmacists during the COVID-19 pandemic

During the COVID-19 pandemic, pharmacists played a crucial role in mitigating the crisis through various activities, including:

1. **Vaccine administration:** Many countries authorised pharmacists to administer COVID-19 vaccines, increasing vaccine coverage and accessibility (FIP, 2021).
2. **Public health education:** Pharmacists provided evidence-based information on preventive measures, combating misinformation and vaccine hesitancy (WHO, 2020).
3. **Screening and testing:** Community pharmacies served as accessible points for COVID-19 testing and symptom screening (CDC, 2021).
4. **Ensuring medicine supply:** Pharmacists managed medicine stocks, preventing shortages and ensuring continued access to essential medicines (World Bank, 2022).
5. **Telehealth and remote consultations:** With movement restrictions in place, pharmacists offered virtual consultations to manage chronic diseases and medication adherence.

## Key public health roles for pharmacists in Zimbabwe

Pharmacists in Zimbabwe can enhance the public health system by contributing in the following areas:

### 1. Disease prevention and health promotion

Pharmacists are well-positioned to lead health promotion campaigns on issues such as:

- Vaccination programmes for diseases like HPV, influenza, and COVID-19 (WHO, 2020).
- Smoking cessation and substance abuse counselling.
- Nutritional advice and weight management programmes (CDC, 2021).
- Sexual and reproductive health education.

### 2. Management of non-communicable diseases (NCDs)

Zimbabwe is experiencing a rise in NCDs such as hypertension, diabetes, and cardiovascular diseases. Pharmacists can contribute by:

- Conducting routine screenings and early detection of NCDs (World Bank, 2022).
- Offering medication therapy management (MTM) services to optimise treatment outcomes (FIP, 2021).
- Educating patients on lifestyle modifications to prevent disease progression (MoHCC, 2021).

### 3. Antimicrobial stewardship (AMS)

With rising antimicrobial resistance (AMR) in Zimbabwe, pharmacists should be empowered to:

- Educate the public on the dangers of antibiotic misuse.
- Guide healthcare providers on appropriate antibiotic prescribing (WHO, 2020).
- Monitor antibiotic consumption trends and contribute to national AMS policies (FIP, 2021).

### 4. Maternal and child health support

Pharmacists can improve maternal and child health through:

- Counselling on prenatal vitamins and medication safety during pregnancy (MoHCC, 2021).
- Providing paediatric medication dosing guidance (CDC, 2021).
- Participating in immunisation programmes for children (WHO, 2020).

### 5. Pharmacovigilance and drug safety monitoring

To enhance medication safety, pharmacists can:

- Report and track adverse drug reactions (ADRs) to improve pharmacovigilance systems (FIP, 2021).
- Educate patients on potential side effects and drug interactions (World Bank, 2022).
- Assist in post-marketing surveillance of medicines.

### 6. Emergency preparedness and disaster response

Beyond pandemics, pharmacists can assist in:

- Stockpiling and distributing essential medicines during natural disasters.
- Offering first aid and trauma care training to communities (WHO, 2020).
- Supporting mental health interventions during crises (FIP, 2021).

## Conclusion

Pharmacists have proven their ability to significantly contribute to public health, as evidenced by their role during the COVID-19 pandemic. By allowing pharmacists to take on expanded public health responsibilities, Zimbabwe can enhance healthcare accessibility, improve disease prevention efforts, and strengthen the overall health system. Policymakers must take decisive action to integrate pharmacists into national public health initiatives, leveraging their expertise to build a healthier and more resilient nation.
